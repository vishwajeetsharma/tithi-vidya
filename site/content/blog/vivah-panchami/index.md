**Vivah Panchami 2026 is on Monday, 14 December.** The Panchami tithi runs from 4:48 pm on Sunday, 13 December, to 7:16 pm on Monday, 14 December, in New Delhi. It marks the wedding of Ram and Sita.

And here's the odd part. It's a wedding festival, yet many families in Mithila and Nepal won't hold their own wedding on it. More on that below.

## Vivah Panchami 2026 date and time

| | New Delhi (IST) | Kathmandu (NPT) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vivah Panchami | Monday, 14 December | Monday, 14 December |
| Panchami begins | 13 Dec, 4:48 pm | 13 Dec, 5:03 pm |
| Panchami ends | 14 Dec, 7:16 pm | 14 Dec, 7:31 pm |

The tithi starts on the evening of the 13th, so why is the festival on the 14th? Because a Hindu calendar day is named by the tithi running at sunrise. On the morning of 14 December, Panchami is running, so that's the day. If tithis are new to you, [here's what a tithi is, with a picture](/blog/tithi-birthday).

The dates come from Tithi Vidya, which uses NASA JPL's [DE440 data](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/de440_de441.html) for the sun and moon.

## Which tithi is Vivah Panchami?

It's **Shukla Panchami**, the 5th tithi of the bright half, in the month of **Margashirsha**. In Nepal and Mithila the month is called Mangsir, or Agahan. Because it follows the moon, its date moves every year, between late November and mid-December.

| Year | Vivah Panchami | Day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2026 | 14 December | Monday |
| 2027 | 3 December | Friday |
| 2028 | 21 November | Tuesday |
| 2029 | 10 December | Monday |
| 2030 | 29 November | Friday |

See how it falls about 11 days earlier most years, then jumps later? That's the lunar calendar catching up with the sun. [Why Hindu dates move like this](/blog/why-your-hindu-birthday-moves) has the full story.

## The story behind Vivah Panchami

In the Ramayana, King Janaka of Mithila set a challenge for anyone who wanted to marry his daughter Sita: lift and string the great bow of Shiva. Prince after prince failed. Then Ram, the young prince of Ayodhya, lifted it and broke it while stringing it. Ram and Sita were married, and tradition holds that the wedding took place on this tithi.

That's why the day is also called Ram Vivah or Sita Vivah.

## How Vivah Panchami is celebrated

The biggest celebration is in **Janakpur**, in Nepal, the old capital of Mithila. Pilgrims arrive from Ayodhya and across India for a wedding procession, the Ram Barat, and the wedding is acted out at the Vivah Mandap near the Janaki temple. [Wikipedia's page on Vivah Panchami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivaha_Panchami) has more on the festival there.

In Ayodhya and in Ram temples elsewhere, priests hold the wedding ceremony for the deities. At home, many families:

- Read the wedding chapter of the Ramcharitmanas or the Ramayana.
- Do a small puja for Ram and Sita.
- Sing traditional wedding songs, especially in Mithila.

## Why some families avoid weddings that day

This surprises a lot of people. In parts of Mithila and Nepal, many families don't fix their own wedding on Vivah Panchami.

The belief comes from Sita's married life. Soon after the wedding came exile, her capture by Ravana, and long separations from Ram. So the folk belief goes that a bride married on this day may face hardship too.

It's a custom, not a rule from scripture, and plenty of families elsewhere see the day as lucky instead. If a wedding date is being chosen in your family, the elders or the family priest will have their own view.
