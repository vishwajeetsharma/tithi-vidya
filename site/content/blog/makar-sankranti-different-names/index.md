**Makar Sankranti has a different name in almost every part of India.** It's Pongal in Tamil Nadu, Lohri and Maghi in Punjab, and Magh Bihu in Assam. It's Uttarayan in Gujarat, Khichdi in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, Poush Sankranti in Bengal, and Maghe Sankranti in Nepal. They all mark one moment: the sun moving into Makar, the sign of Capricorn.

That makes it unusual. Almost every other Hindu festival follows the moon, so [its date jumps around by weeks](/blog/why-your-hindu-birthday-moves). Makar Sankranti follows the sun, so it sits on 14 or 15 January. In 2027 it's the 15th for most calendars, and I'll show you why with real numbers.

## Makar Sankranti names in different states

Here are 12 names for the same festival, with what each region is known for. Everything in this table comes from Wikipedia's pages on [Makar Sankranti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makar_Sankranti) and its regional festivals.

| Name | Where | What people do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pongal | Tamil Nadu | Three or four days: Bhogi, Thai Pongal, Mattu Pongal and Kaanum Pongal. Rice is boiled with milk and jaggery. |
| Lohri | Punjab | A bonfire on the night before Maghi |
| Maghi | Punjab | The day after Lohri |
| Magh Bihu (Bhogali Bihu) | Assam | A harvest festival. Uruka, the night before, has a night feast, and the meji bonfire is lit at dawn. |
| Uttarayan | Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh | Named for the sun's journey north. Kite flying is a big part of the day. |
| Khichdi | Uttar Pradesh, Bihar | Khichdi is cooked, eaten and offered, including to Guru Gorakhnath |
| Sakraat | Rajasthan | Makar Sankranti by its local name |
| Poush Sankranti | West Bengal | The Gangasagar Mela, where the Ganga meets the Bay of Bengal |
| Sankranthi, or Peddha Panduga | Andhra Pradesh, Telangana | Kept over several days, starting with Bhogi |
| Makaravilakku | Kerala | The festival's name in Kerala |
| Shishur Senkrath | Kashmir | The Kashmiri name |
| Maghe Sankranti | Nepal | The first day of the month Magh |

In Karnataka it's kept over several days, much like Pongal, with a dish called ellu. In Maharashtra, people share til-gul, sweets of sesame and jaggery.

## Why one festival has so many names

Two reasons stand out.

**It starts a new month.** In calendars that follow the sun, the sun's move into a new sign starts a new month. So the festival often takes the new month's name. Thai Pongal is the [first day of the Tamil month Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pongal_%28festival%29). Maghe Sankranti is [the first day of Magh in Nepal's calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghe_Sankranti). And Punjab's Maghi, the day after Lohri, is named for Magh too.

**It's a harvest festival.** The winter crop is in, and many regions celebrate with what it gives. There's new rice for Pongal, a feast for Bhogali Bihu, and khichdi in the north.

> **Did you know?** Sesame (til) is one of the most important foods of Makar Sankranti, Wikipedia notes. So the sweets change name from state to state, but they're nearly all sesame and jaggery: til-gul in Maharashtra, ellu in Karnataka, and til rice for Lohri.

## Why it's on 14 or 15 January

A Hindu festival that follows the moon moves by about 11 days a year, then jumps back. It's the same drift that moves your Hindu birthday around the calendar. Makar Sankranti doesn't, because a solar year is the same length as the calendar year, almost.

That "almost" matters. I worked out the moment the sun enters Makar for a few years, from NASA JPL's [DE440 data](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/de440_de441.html) with the Lahiri ayanamsa, the one most Indian calendars use:

| Year | The sun enters Makar (IST) | Usual festival day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2025 | Tuesday, 14 January, 8:55 am | 14 January |
| 2026 | Wednesday, 14 January, 3:07 pm | 14 January |
| 2027 | Thursday, 14 January, 9:10 pm | 15 January |
| 2028 | Saturday, 15 January, 3:24 am | 15 January |

See the pattern? The moment comes about 6 hours later each year, then a leap day pulls it back. In 2027 it comes at 9:10 pm, after sunset everywhere in India, so many calendars keep the festival the next day, Friday, 15 January. (Our [Hindu calendar 2027](/blog/hindu-calendar) does too, with Lohri on Thursday, 14 January.)

It's also creeping later over the centuries. The same Wikipedia page notes that Makar Sankranti will fall on 16 January for the first time in 2102.

## In Nepal: Maghe Sankranti

In Nepal the festival is Maghe Sankranti, also called Maghi, on the first day of the month Magh in the Bikram Sambat calendar. It's a major festival of the Magar community, and the Tharu people celebrate it as the start of their new year.

In 2027 the sun enters Makar at 9:25 pm Nepal time on Thursday, 14 January, well after sunset in Kathmandu (5:29 pm). Nepal's own calendar sets which day is 1 Magh, so check it for the exact date.

## Common questions

### What is Makar Sankranti called in different states?

Pongal in Tamil Nadu, Lohri and Maghi in Punjab, Magh Bihu in Assam, and Uttarayan in Gujarat. Khichdi in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, Sakraat in Rajasthan, and Poush Sankranti in West Bengal. Peddha Panduga in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, Makaravilakku in Kerala and Shishur Senkrath in Kashmir. In Nepal it's Maghe Sankranti.

### Is Lohri the same as Makar Sankranti?

Not quite. Lohri is the bonfire night before it. In Punjab the next day, Maghi, is the Sankranti itself. In 2027, Lohri is on Thursday, 14 January, by most calendars.

### Is Pongal the same as Makar Sankranti?

It's the same festival in Tamil Nadu, kept over three or four days. Thai Pongal, the main day, is the first day of the Tamil month Thai.

### Why is Makar Sankranti on 15 January in 2027?

The sun enters Makar at 9:10 pm IST on Thursday, 14 January, after sunset. So many calendars move the festival to the next morning, Friday, 15 January.

### What is Makar Sankranti called in Nepal?

Maghe Sankranti, or Maghi. It falls on the first day of the Nepali month Magh.
